    Alignment with the Kafe DevKit evaluation proposal¶

    This document maps the supplied evaluation proposal to Tutor behavior and evidence that a human evaluator can review. It is a study preparation guide, not evidence of educational effectiveness. The proposal describes two university groups, approximately 40–60 available participants, and a minimum of 84 minutes: a 12-minute pretest, two 30-minute practical activities and a 12-minute posttest. Instructions, informed consent and the usability/perception instruments require additional time.

    The current intended pretest and posttest each have six short-answer questions: three ML and three DL, scored 0–2 each, maximum 12. The user confirmed this version against the revised proposal's six-row content table, 12-point rubric and supplied form fragments; the proposal's remaining eight-question wording is stale. Earlier repository descriptions of eight one-point items are superseded. The eight conceptual topics below remain broader teaching coverage, not questionnaire items.

    This alignment uses the supplied proposal's main document text, form HTML fragments, SUS/upload screenshots and architecture diagram. The originals remain unchanged. DOCX text was extracted, but rendering was unavailable; no page/layout verification is claimed. No live form or image endpoint was visited or submitted. Three questions per form reference images whose data were not supplied; their contents and equivalent difficulty remain unknown. The fragments say to select “No sé” while offering short-text controls without that selectable option. The research team must reconcile instructions, accessibility, missing image data, version pairing and scoring reliability before administration. No item text or answer key is bundled into production guidance.

    Conceptual traceability¶

    Objective from the proposal Appropriate Tutor support during practical work Observable evidence and interpretation limits
    ML 1. Sensitivity Explain sensitivity/recall as the fraction of actual positive cases detected, TP / (TP + FN). Establish which label denotes fraud and connect missed fraud to false negatives. Interpret available observed or learner-reported values. Learner explains a confusion matrix or recall calculation and its relevance to fraud. A numerical metric alone does not show that the learner understands it.
    ML 2. Comparing classifiers Help compare the supplied starter classifier, logistic regression and Gaussian Naive Bayes on the same held-out cases. Explain competing criteria and invite a justified choice. The .kf program, comparable results and the learner's conclusion identify the comparison and rationale. The Tutor must not choose the final submitted answer for the learner.
    ML 3. Imbalanced data Explain why predicting the majority class can produce high accuracy while missing fraud. Discuss class counts, recall, precision and the consequences of false positives and false negatives. Learner relates class imbalance to the reported metrics. An accuracy-only ranking is insufficient evidence of minority-class detection. API options must follow the reviewed MACHINE contracts.
    ML 4. Evaluation with new data Explain training versus held-out evaluation and why repeated use of the training cases does not establish generalization. Keep preprocessing fitted on training data when explaining a valid evaluation workflow. Distinct training/evaluation data and the learner's explanation support the observation. A successful run or a high training score cannot establish performance on unseen transactions.
    DL 1. Interpreting predictions Connect the four XOR inputs to their expected labels, then help interpret the model's actual outputs, shapes and documented output activation. Distinguish a numeric prediction from the rule used to assign a class. Learner compares predictions with XOR targets and explains discrepancies. The Tutor must not invent predictions or infer success from code that has not been run.
    DL 2. Representation capacity Explain why one linear decision boundary cannot separate XOR and why hidden layers require nonlinear transformations to change that capacity. Invite the learner's architectural reasoning. The learner's diagram, explanation or controlled architecture experiment supports interpretation. More layers alone do not guarantee successful training.
    DL 3. Learning rate Explain the effect of update size, unstable training and slow convergence. Support a controlled experiment that distinguishes learning rate, epochs, architecture and initialization. Recorded settings and observed runs support a comparison. A different seed or more epochs cannot by itself repair the representational limit of a single linear separator.
    DL 4. Overfitting Explain fitting observed cases versus generalization, and the limits of conclusions from training loss alone. Learner identifies what additional held-out evidence would be needed. The four XOR truth-table cases describe the complete stated Boolean task; fitting those cases alone does not estimate generalization to a separate population or demonstrate overfitting. The evaluator must define any additional evaluation data and claim.

    Reviewed positional APIs, input shapes and implementation limits are documented in the MACHINE contracts and GESHA API coverage. References support explanations; repository examples and their expected outputs are not observations of a participant's program.

    KAFE metric distinction: machine.recall_score computes macro recall over the classes present in the reference labels. It does not automatically report sensitivity for the fraud-positive class, and there is no average= option in the current positional DSL API. Establish the positive label and the confusion-matrix class order before interpreting TP and FN. For a chosen class with no actual positive cases, TP / (TP + FN) is undefined; distinguish that mathematical limit from the API's documented zero-denominator convention.

    Practical activities and learner participation¶

    For the fraud activity, the learner executes and interprets the supplied starter classifier, explores logistic regression and Gaussian Naive Bayes, compares all three and writes an individual conclusion. The Tutor should explain unfamiliar concepts and available KAFE calls, provide bounded hints or illustrative fragments, and respond to the learner's own reasoning. It may calculate and organize supplied evidence directly while leaving the final assessed comparison and conclusion to the learner. It must not replace the activity with a ready-to-submit program or a final model choice. The proposal does not identify the starter classifier by name and includes no starter program or dataset. The separately attributed facilitator package below supplies new starting materials; its shallow tree is not the proposal's original unnamed model.

    For the XOR activity, the learner begins with one neuron, examines the results and considers the proposal's three alternatives: more epochs, a different learning rate or added layers. Initialization seed is an additional controlled-training condition, not a fourth alternative listed in the proposal. The Tutor should clarify the distinction between representational capacity and training behavior while leaving the proposed experiment and justification to the learner. An affine threshold cannot represent XOR; composing only affine layers remains affine, while nonlinear hidden transformations change capacity without guaranteeing convergence. It may help diagnose the learner's implementation without supplying the complete exercise solution. Stochastic training outcomes must be described as observations of particular settings, not promised results.

    Meaningful participation can be sought in questions, proposed alternatives, decisions, changed settings, comparisons, checks and individual explanations. A checkpoint confirmation records adoption of a displayed choice; a Tutor-written summary is an unconfirmed interpretation. Neither is independent evidence of learner reasoning or mastery. Explanations, sketches, pseudocode and concise technical answers can all be relevant evidence; the final research rubric must specify how evaluators judge them.

    One guided Tutor offers direct concept explanations and focused reasoning at meaningful choices. Requests for shorter or fuller explanations affect presentation within eligible bounded conversation history; they create no durable style memory or inferred ability. Paused teaching removes additional reasoning requirements; it does not authorize writing files, executing a program or supplying a complete assessment answer. Direct concept questions deserve a useful explanation rather than an obligatory quiz. The Tutor should reuse already supplied answers and metrics instead of repeatedly asking for them.

    The engineering target now includes first-semester learners, whereas the proposal describes second- and third-semester groups. Readiness guidance should use familiar language, explain before asking, offer one manageable next step and correct the learner's actual misconception. A short explanation, sketch or draft can already supply useful reasoning; feedback should affirm supported claims and identify an actual gap only if present. This target does not establish that either activity can be completed by novices within its 30-minute allocation. Executable starting materials are prepared; first-use orientation, classroom runtime/provider setup and a facilitated novice pilot remain prerequisites for a classroom readiness claim.

    Facilitator classroom materials¶

    A separate local deliverable, kafe-classroom-starters.zip, contains seven files: Spanish instructions, DATA-NOTICE.md, provenance.json, separate training/evaluation CSVs, fraud-baseline.kf and xor-one-neuron.kf. The independently reviewed archive is 490,107 bytes, SHA256 96819195821a0dbf829a85138349badf3a962f980619daaedb3a85893ff03e9d. It is a facilitator handoff, with no public download URL or bundled interpreter. It contains one shallow-tree implementation and the one-neuron XOR starting point; the other classifier implementations, final network and individual conclusions remain learner work. Teacher programs/results and original source-row IDs stay outside the learner archive.

    The historical anonymized ULB/Worldline credit-card source comes through OpenML dataset 1597, with original publisher attribution and ODbL 1.0 database / DbCL 1.0 contents notices retained. A full chronological 80/20 source split precedes fixed seed-42 sampling. The resulting 1,600 training and 400 evaluation rows contain 80 and 20 frauds respectively: a disclosed 5% didactic enrichment, rather than the source prevalence or current banking conditions. Predictors are V1–V28 and Amount; Class, Time and row identifiers are excluded. Scaling is learned from training only. No selected predictor vector crosses partitions; repeated vectors remain within training. Upstream PCA cannot be refitted or audited from the available source, and 20 evaluation frauds limit sensitivity resolution to five percentage points per case. The data notice records these limitations and redistribution terms separately from KAFE's software license.

    Retained actual teacher CLI outputs were independently checked beyond exit status, including every predicted fraud label, matrix axes, counts and empty diagnostic streams. The baseline works from the repository and extracted-kit working directories. The three particular fraud runs and one-neuron/nonlinear XOR checks establish executable feasibility with fixed inputs and settings; they do not choose a learner's model or guarantee convergence, generalization, lesson timing or educational effectiveness. The archive is not an extension installation, runtime deployment or research-data upload. Facilitators must prepare the intended machines and preserve the complete extracted folder before a supervised novice pilot.

    Honest execution evidence¶

    A learner-started Run can provide private, bounded stdout, stderr, an exit outcome, truncation information and runtime/source observations. For the currently authorized active file, the composer includes that observation in the provider snapshot and getLatestRunResult reads the captured result. A metadata label such as “not a correctness check” does not mean that output is absent. The Tutor can interpret available metric values without reproducing the raw output block as its response.

    If the learner supplies numbers in a message, the Tutor may explain and compare them as learner-reported values. This does not authorize a claim that the extension observed a Run. If relevant metrics are absent, it should explain what can already be inferred and ask only for the indispensable missing values. Missing output does not justify inventing results, repeatedly requesting values already present, or refusing all conceptual help.

    Runs from another active file do not supply raw output for the current file. When the same file has changed, earlier output can remain available with explicit historical/changed-source labels; it does not verify the edited code. The host separately observes saved-file boundaries and the current editor contents, and does not claim exact executed bytes. New, switched and restored conversations clear private Run evidence and executable permissions. The combined private stdout/stderr bound remains 1 MiB, and restricted workspaces do not authorize file or Run tools.

    An observed exit code is not a correctness verdict. A passing example, a learner explanation, a confirmation click or a Tutor response cannot establish mastery, learning gains or the causal efficacy of Kafe DevKit. Automated regression tests check context transport, provenance, bounds and instructions; they do not establish that every live provider response follows the pedagogy.

    Development-only activity-dialogues.json supplies two three-turn Spanish rehearsals with synthetic observations, internal mathematical oracles and actual-answer review criteria. Production coordinator/composer tests submit only exact learner text and retain eligible whole pairs; rubrics stay offline. Canned replies verify transport and authority boundaries only. Three six-turn Flash/Pro samples were rejected, the guided-7 six-plus-two round was mixed, and the final seven-reply 9c25d04 sample was not accepted: fraud comparison and bounded constructor help coexist with material XOR baseline, evidence-origin, isolation and draft-feedback failures. The user chose to keep this checkpoint on 2026-10-28 and stop further model/reasoning experiments. The full answer-quality goal remains incomplete; Pro is an experimental comparison default without a winner or student-readiness claim. The 33 tuning requests and earlier separate four-message pilot establish no novice comprehension, lesson completion or learning result. Source 9c25d04 passed exact-revision real VS Code 1.96 trusted/untrusted CI and packaging; that engineering gate is separate from empirical novice efficacy and final documentation-checkpoint delivery. See Tutor reliability for exact rounds, limitations and receipts.

    Collection, export and consent¶

    The proposal asks for the participant's .kf program, execution results when available, an individual conclusion and Tutor interactions when there is interaction and authorization to analyze it. Keep these as distinguishable evidence sources. Execution results may require a separately authorized collection method because private host Run evidence is deliberately absent from saved/exported transcripts.

    Each workspace can retain independent local conversations. The learner explicitly selects a conversation and exports it as versioned UTF-8 JSON through a native Save dialog. Export contains visible messages, statuses and safe summaries, including text or code the learner typed. It excludes the unsent draft, preferences, provider history/dependencies, attached source snapshots, private Run evidence, credentials and executable handles. A visible learner message that quotes results or includes identifying text remains part of the export; export does not promise automatic anonymization.

    Saving a local conversation or opening the export dialog is not research consent. The research team must obtain informed consent, define which artifacts may be analyzed, establish participant identifiers and handling/retention rules, and review shared copies for identifying information. The extension does not automatically upload transcripts to a research repository, generate participation scores or supply an efficacy dashboard. Provider requests made during assistance are separate from any research collection; participants must receive the applicable information before participating. See the extension guide for context and conversation controls.

    Independent measurement and human procedures¶

    The proposal requires individual pretest and posttest responses without documentation, Internet or AI assistance. KAFE Tutor should be unavailable for those sessions through facilitator procedure; the extension has no proctoring mode that enforces questionnaire conditions. Practical activities allow Kafe DevKit, official documentation and KAFE Tutor, while the proposal excludes external AI assistants. The facilitator owns that distinction and its communication.

    Research item Status represented by the proposal Work still owned by the research team
    Six-item conceptual pretest and posttest Six short-answer items per form, three ML and three DL; human rubric 0–2 each, maximum 12. Resolve the remaining source/form mismatches, missing images and accessibility; review keys, coverage, equivalent difficulty, scoring consistency, individual pairing and independent administration. Analyze actual collected scores.
    Practical performance rubric Construction, execution, interpretation, checking and justification are proposed. Finalize observable anchors and evaluator procedure; pilot before claiming comparable scores.
    Adapted ICAP observation Passive, Active, Constructive, Interactive and Not evaluable categories; three evidence sources and descriptive/exploratory analysis are specified. Human evaluators apply the procedure below, document classification decisions and assess agreement. No empirical validity or reliability result is supplied.
    Tutor accompaniment Pedagogical accompaniment remains an intended dimension. A completed dedicated accompaniment rubric is not established by its mention; define/review criteria and human judgment procedure.
    System Usability Scale (SUS) Ten ordered statements, five response options and the standard contribution calculation are specified; administration follows both practical activities. Preserve instrument order and authorized language/version; apply the procedure below to actual responses. Language-version validation and participant scores are not established.

    Adapted ICAP observation¶

    At the end of both activities, the proposal combines three sources: the individual's brief decision record (strategies, tests and justifications), developed .kf solutions with execution results, and authorized Tutor interactions. Human review produces a profile per learner and activity, identifying observable behaviors:

    Category Operational interpretation
    Passive Receives information or observes results without observable manipulation or elaboration.
    Active Executes, applies or manipulates supplied information without new explanation or inference.
    Constructive Generates an explanation, hypothesis, inference or justified decision beyond the supplied information.
    Interactive Substantive reciprocal exchange develops, questions or revises ideas; the learner contributes reasoning.
    Not evaluable Evidence is insufficient to classify the process, even if a final artifact exists.

    Message counts, a Run, a confirmation or Tutor-written summaries do not determine classification. Interactive participation requires evidence of reciprocal construction. Report category distributions and descriptive ML/DL comparisons; relationships with practical performance and pre/post changes are exploratory. The adapted rubric alone cannot demonstrate deep understanding or general autonomy. No automatic ICAP score/classification is implemented, and inter-rater agreement remains to be established.

    SUS administration and interpretation¶

    The supplied procedure administers ten ordered statements through Google Forms after both practical activities, evaluating the complete experienced environment: VS Code, KAFE and KAFE Tutor. Preserve the ten items, their order and five options coded 1–5. Human analysis uses response minus 1 for odd-numbered items and 5 minus response for even-numbered items; sum the contributions and multiply by 2.5 for a 0–100 score. This is a scale score, not a percentage of correct answers. The extension does not administer or automatically score it.

    Report valid questionnaire count, mean and standard deviation, median and interquartile range, distributions and academic-group summaries where group size permits. No actual responses or SUS result are supplied. After a brief experience SUS describes perceived usability; it does not establish timing efficiency, accessibility, absence of technical errors or pedagogical effectiveness. Interpret it alongside practical and participation evidence. Voluntary participation, email registration and a later transcript upload each require the research team's distinct information/consent procedure; none follows from local storage or export.

    For real paired questionnaire data, the proposal defines individual change as posttest score minus pretest score and proposes descriptive summaries, ML/DL differences and item-level analysis. The suitability of a paired statistical analysis and an effect estimate remains a research decision based on the collected data and study design. No participant data, empirical learning effect, usability score or efficacy conclusion is supplied by this alignment document.
